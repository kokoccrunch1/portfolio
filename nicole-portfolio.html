Context

NTUC Health operates across several healthcare service lines in Singapore, each with its own staff, workflows, and digital touchpoints. Designing for this organisation means designing for a web of users whose needs often conflict — a care coordinator needs speed and scannability; an administrator needs configurability and accuracy; a healthcare worker needs trust that the system will not let them down. My work was to make each of those experiences clear and workable, across four different team contexts simultaneously.

How I worked

Across all seven projects, I followed a consistent design process — though the emphasis at each stage varied depending on what the PM and team already knew about their users.

01
Understand the user
Interviews, walkthroughs of existing tools, and PM-briefing sessions to build a working model of who I was designing for
02
Define & scope
Working with PMs to bound the problem, identify constraints, and agree on the right measure of success
03
Design
User flows → wireframes → high-fidelity Figma screens with full interface state coverage
04
Review & test
Prototype walkthroughs with business units; iterative design reviews with PMs and front-end engineers
05
Handoff
Implementation-ready Figma with annotated states, edge cases, and component specs for engineering

Working within constraints

Across four teams simultaneously, I was always designing within constraints I didn't set — existing component libraries, fixed technical integration points, engineering sprint capacity. Rather than treating these as blockers, I used them as creative inputs that kept the work grounded in what could actually ship.

Multi-actor workflows

When one interface serves multiple user roles, every design decision has downstream consequences. I learned to hold the full actor map in mind — not only how the primary user would interact, but how data entered by one role would be read and acted upon by another downstream. This made me a more systemic designer.

About

Trained to see
people and systems

My degree was in Sociology and Data, Design and Communications at SMU. That combination wasn't incidental — it trained me to pay attention to people in context: not just their behaviour at the screen, but the operational environment they're navigating, the decisions they're trying to make, and the larger systems they're embedded in.

I currently work at NHG Health, where I support the Chief Wellness Officer in implementing well-being frameworks across 7 public healthcare institutions and ~25,000 staff. This role has given me a ground-level understanding of how Singapore's public healthcare system actually works — its competing priorities, its operational complexity, and the gap between policy intent and frontline reality.

I'm now looking to bring that understanding back into product design — where I can translate what I've seen into interfaces that make clinical environments more navigable, and that empower the people working within them to do their jobs well.
